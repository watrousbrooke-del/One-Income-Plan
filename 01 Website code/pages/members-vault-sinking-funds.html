The Sinking Fund System

Small pots of money set aside on purpose, so a set of tires stops taking the whole month down with it.

20 min to set up 3 printables 1 short training
Get the three pages
System 02
◆

Most budget emergencies are not emergencies. They are bills we forgot were coming.

Christmas is in December every year. The car always needs tires eventually. School shoes, the dentist, the birthday party. None of these are surprises, but on one income they land like surprises because nothing was set aside for them.

A sinking fund is a small pot of money with one job. You put a little in every payday, and when the thing arrives, the money is already sitting there. It is the single biggest change in how a month feels, and it starts with one fund, not twelve.

How to do it

Setting up your first fund.

Twenty minutes, once. Then it runs itself on payday.

1

Name the surprise that hurt most this year.

Car repair, Christmas, a medical bill, school costs. The one that made you reach for the credit card. That is your first fund.

2 minutes
2

Put a price and a date on it.

Roughly what it will cost and roughly when. Christmas at four hundred dollars in December. Tires at six hundred sometime next year. A rough guess is far better than no number.

3 minutes
3

Let the calculator split it.

Open the Monthly Contribution Calculator, type in the amount and the months until you need it. It tells you what to set aside each month and each payday.

5 minutes
4

Give the fund a home.

A separate savings account is easiest, and most banks let you open several and name them. An envelope works too. What matters is that this money is not sitting in checking where it gets spent by accident.

5 minutes
5

Add it to your payday page.

It becomes one more line on the Payday Reset Page. Set the transfer to repeat automatically if your bank allows it.

3 minutes
6

Colour in your progress.

Every payday, update the tracker. Watching a fund fill up is surprisingly motivating, and it is the part people tell me they look forward to.

2 minutes
Which fund first

When you cannot start them all, start here.

In this order, for most one-income families. Skip any that do not fit your house.

first

The car fund

Repairs, tires, registration. For most families the car is the most common surprise and the most expensive one to put on a card.

second

Christmas and birthdays

The surprise that is not a surprise. Divide last year’s total by twelve and you have your monthly number.

third

Medical and dental

Copays, prescriptions, the dentist. Small amounts that add up and always arrive at the worst time.

fourth

Home repairs

The water heater, the washer, the leak under the sink. Renters too, for the deposit and moving costs.

fifth

Kids and school

Shoes, supplies, field trips, sports fees. The School Supply Plan in the Printables library pairs with this one.

then

The other seven

Clothing, gifts, insurance deductibles, pets, annual subscriptions, travel, and a fun fund. Add one at a time, only when the last one feels normal.

When it slips

The questions everyone asks about this one.

I do not have anything left over to put in a fund.

Start with five dollars. The amount matters far less than the habit. A five dollar fund that exists is better than a hundred dollar fund that does not. If even five feels impossible, go back to the payday page, because something there wants looking at first.

Something broke and the fund was not full yet. Now what?

Use what is there. That is what it is for. Half the cost on the card is better than all of it on the card. Then start filling it again from the next payday, with no guilt attached.

Should I pay off debt first or start sinking funds?

Both, a little. Without a fund, the next surprise goes straight back on the card you are paying off. One small car fund protects the debt progress you are making.

How many accounts do I actually need?

One is enough to start. Many banks let you split one savings account into named buckets. If yours does not, a simple list on the tracker of how much of the balance belongs to which fund works just as well.
