The most common way to fail at this is to start everything at once.

I see it every time. A new member opens all seven systems on a Saturday, prints forty pages, and by the following Thursday has done none of them. Not because she is not capable, but because nobody can change seven things at once.

This system is the order. One thing a week, in a sequence that builds on itself. And for the weeks when life goes sideways, the fifteen minute path, so you never fall all the way back to the start.

How to do it

How to add one system at a time.

The same five steps for every system in the vault.

1

Pick one.

The next one on the roadmap, or the one Quick Start picked for you. Write it at the top of the checklist.

2

Print only its pages.

Not the whole library. Just the pages for this one system. Less paper on the counter means more of it gets used.

3

Use it for one full payday or one full week.

That is the definition of done. Not read, not understood. Used.

4

Mark it as running.

Move it to the running column on the checklist. Running means it happens without you having to remember to do it.

5

Then, and only then, add the next.

If the last one has slipped, go back to it first. There is no prize for speed in here.

When it slips

The questions everyone asks about this one.

I already started several systems at once. Should I stop?

Pick the one that is working best and keep it. Pause the rest without guilt. They will be here, and they will go better one at a time.

Can I do them in a different order?

Yes. The order suits most families, but if groceries are the fire in your house, start there. The rule is one at a time, not this exact sequence.

A month got away from me and I stopped everything.

Do the fifteen minute path this week. Next payday, pick up the budget system again. Nothing in here expires and nobody is keeping score.

How long until all seven are running?

Most families take two to four months. That is not slow. That is how it sticks.
