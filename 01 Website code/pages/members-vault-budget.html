01 for: no idea where it goes

The One-Income Budget System

The fifteen minute payday routine that tells you where every dollar is going, without tracking every penny.

You do not have a spending problem. You have a visibility problem.

When money feels out of control on one income, it is almost never because you are careless. It is because the money moves faster than you can see it. Bills land in the wrong week, the card gets used for groceries, and by the middle of the month you are guessing.

This system does one thing. Every payday, before you spend a dollar, you sit down for fifteen minutes and give every dollar of that paycheck a job. That is it. No apps, no tracking every coffee, no spreadsheet you will abandon by March. It is how we paid off $34,000 on one income, one payday at a time.

How to do it

The payday routine, start to finish.

Do this on payday, before groceries, before anything. Pour a coffee first.

1

Write down what actually landed.

Not what the paycheck should be. What is in the account right now. That number goes at the top of the Payday Reset Page.

1 minute
2

Cover the bills this paycheck owns.

Look at the Bill Due Date Calendar. Every bill due before the next payday comes out of this paycheck. Write each one down and subtract it. If you have never filled in the calendar, do it now: list every bill and the date it comes out, then mark which payday covers it.

4 minutes
3

Set aside groceries and gas for the stretch.

One number for groceries, one for gas, to last until the next payday. Pick the number on purpose. If you do not know it yet, use what you spent last month and adjust next time.

2 minutes
4

Feed one sinking fund, even a small one.

Five dollars counts. This is the line that stops the next surprise from wrecking the month. When you are ready, the Sinking Fund System shows you how to set the right amount.

1 minute
5

Give every leftover dollar a job.

Extra debt payment, savings, a little fun money. What matters is that the leftover is decided now, on paper, instead of disappearing on a Tuesday. If the number is negative, that is not failure. It is information, and it is exactly what this page is for.

4 minutes
6

Copy the totals onto the monthly page.

Once a month is enough. The One-Page Monthly Budget shows planned against actual, so you can see which line keeps surprising you. Colour in your debt squares while you are there.

3 minutes
Before your first payday

Three things to do once, then never again.

Ten minutes this week, and every payday after this gets faster.

one

List every bill

Rent or mortgage, utilities, phone, insurance, car, subscriptions, minimum debt payments. Write the amount and the date it leaves your account.

two

Mark which payday covers what

On the Bill Due Date Calendar, put each bill under the payday that comes before it. This is the step that ends the “why is the account empty on the 14th” feeling.

three

Pick your payday time

Same time, same chair, every payday. Put it in your phone with an alarm. The routine works because you do not have to decide to do it.

When it slips

The questions everyone asks about this one.

What if the money runs out before the bills do?

Then this page has done its job, because now you know before the bill bounces instead of after. Call the company before the due date and ask to move it, write down which bills can flex, and bring the page to the next live session. We will work through it together, real numbers and all.

I get paid irregularly. Does this still work?

Yes. Budget the paycheck you have, not the one you expect. Cover the bills due before your next likely payday, and if a check comes in bigger, the extra goes to the next bill in line before it goes anywhere else.

My husband does not want to do this with me.

That is common, and it is fine. Do it on your own for two paydays. When the month feels calmer, show him one page, not all four. The numbers usually do the convincing.

I missed a payday. Do I start over?

No. Nothing in here expires. Reprint the page and do the next payday. One missed payday is a normal week in a house with kids.
