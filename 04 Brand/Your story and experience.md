## Background: how she grew up

- "Brooke did not grow up with money." Parents struggled, no savings, no cushion. She remembers them writing checks, then trying to make the money to cover them before they cleared. "Money always felt behind, like it was already spent before it ever came in." She quietly promised herself she would not live that way. (BMG 1.4, Q83)
- Short story version Genasys wrote from it: "I grew up knowing the stress of living paycheck to paycheck." (BMG 1.4)

## Turning points (chronological, as best the docs allow)

1. **The kitchen table conversation.** She and her husband talked honestly about living on one income "not as a temporary experiment, but as a real possibility." Nervous, aware of the sacrifices, "quiet, thoughtful, and rooted in faith." (BMG 1.4, Q81)
2. **The pregnancy decision.** Staying home was non-negotiable. (ESSAY)
3. **The hard years.** Saying "we do not have the money" to friends until they stopped asking; friendships "slowly dwindled because we were not willing to keep up with what everyone else was doing." Friends bought boats and campers, went to waterparks; "we were saying no while everyone around us was saying yes." (ESSAY)
4. **The debt.** "At one point, we were over thirty-four thousand dollars in debt." (ESSAY)
5. **The pay cut season.** Husband working 60+ hours a week, "often doing the job of two people," while the economy struggled. Company gave him a choice: take a pay cut or be laid off. He took the pay cut. They "cut expenses everywhere they could, tracked every dollar, and stayed united." (BMG 1.4, Q84) [ESSAY says 50 to 60 hours at $10/hr; BMG says 60+. Probably different seasons, unconfirmed.]
6. **Financial Peace University.** "Friends invited us to Dave Ramsey's Financial Peace University at church. That course changed everything. Not because our income changed, but because we finally had a system." (ESSAY)
7. **The last credit card payment.** "I remember looking at our budget for the following month and realizing we suddenly had an extra two hundred and fifty dollars that was not going to credit card companies anymore. And I knew exactly what to do with it. That is when it hit me: 'Wait. I actually know what I am doing.'" (ESSAY)
8. **Emergency fund first, then the car loans, then a car bought with cash.** (SITE about.html; not found in the primary docs I read, possibly from VCQ. Verify.)
9. **Budget failures before it stuck.** "Failed at sticking to a budget more times than she can count." Plans looked good on paper but did not fit real life. "Now she knows the plan was the problem, not her." (BMG 1.4, Q80)

## Her stories (the storytelling vault)

Ready-made story seeds. Primary source in brackets.

- **The bounced check.** "I bounced a check once. Not because I was careless, but because I truly thought the money was there." Did the math, forgot to write something down. Embarrassing and humbling. (BMG Q76)
- **The budget failure confession.** Plans that looked good on paper; she thought she was the problem; the plan was. (BMG Q80)
- **The meal-stretching disaster.** Stretched a meal too far, "technically counted as dinner but nobody was impressed," everyone looked at their plates and laughed, they ordered pizza. (BMG Q79)
- **The library weeks.** Library every week in two local towns, free kids' programs and craft days. Walks to the animal park. Walking to the school for free summer lunch and the playground. "These literally were the best days before the kids started school." "The kids were never bored. They never felt deprived." (ESSAY)
- **The kids' gratitude.** Kids casually said they were grateful for their simple life, memories, meals at home, time together. Caught her off guard. (BMG Q82)
- **The childcare win-win.** Watched a friend's child for about a third of what the friend paid in daycare; it covered a week of groceries. (BMG Q87)
- **The camper season.** Friends shared their camper so the family could still take trips, splitting camping costs. (BMG Q88)
- **Cars with cash.** Two cars bought with cash; the driveway moment. (BMG Q89)
- **Christmas Eve breakfast.** The family does breakfast or brunch on Christmas Eve instead of a big dinner. Takes the pressure off. (BMG Q78)
- **The kitchen table values moment.** Simple home-cooked meal, talking through the day, "togetherness over busyness, gratitude over comparison." (BMG Q77)
- **The expensive years.** Middle and high school sports, special shoes and equipment; "We would put whatever the kids needed in the budget and make it work." Garage sales funded extras. "I do not know how we were able to swing all the expenses we had, but we did it." (ESSAY)
- **The move to the country.** Bigger house for the same money. (ESSAY)
- **Living proof.** "People think it is not possible to live on one income. But we are living proof that you can, and comfortably." (ESSAY)
