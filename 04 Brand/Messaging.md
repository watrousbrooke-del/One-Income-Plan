## Positioning

**Strategic positioning statement (BMG 1.5):** "For one-income families who are overwhelmed by financial stress and tired of advice that feels extreme, rigid, or shame-driven, The One Income Plan is the faith-centered intentional living resource that replaces pressure with peace through simple, sustainable habits. Unlike traditional budgeting blogs that focus on strict rules and constant tracking, or hustle-culture influencers who glorify extremes, The One Income Plan acknowledges the emotional side of money and builds systems around real life, not perfection."

**The four-part "onlyness" (BMG 1.6):** faith-centered ◆ emotional-first ◆ simple systems ◆ one-income specialization ("not adapted from dual-income advice").

**Brooke's own gap statement (WB 3.3):** "Unlike others in my niche who teach generic budgeting tips, I focus on one-income families building simple, steady rhythms, because I believe financial peace and freedom are absolutely possible, no matter how much you make."

**People come to her when they're tired of** "feeling behind every month, starting over, and wondering why budgeting never sticks" **and ready to** "feel calm, confident, and in control, living well on one income with practical, peaceful routines that actually work." (WB 3.3)

**White space she sees (WB 3.3):** "No one is guiding families through the emotional and practical reality of thriving on one income. Everyone talks about cutting expenses, but no one teaches how to build peaceful habits that make a one-income lifestyle sustainable and rewarding."

**The one message the market needs (WB 3.3):** "You don't need two incomes to live a good life. You just need a plan that works for the one you have."

**Integration angle (OSQ Q23, WB 1.3):** money, meals, and home rhythms taught together. Core topic: "One-Income Living Systems (Budget + Meals + Home)." "Money doesn't exist in a vacuum."

**Competitors Brooke studied (WB 3.3, WB 3.1):** Budget Minded Mama, Budgeting for Bliss, Fun Cheap or Free (Jordan Page), The Budget Mom. Her read: they are generic, numbers-and-tips, less emotional, not one-income specific, bright pastel or aspirational.

## Contrarian takes and hot takes (BMG Appendix K, AIP)

- Frugal living does not need to feel extreme or miserable to work. (Q46)
- More information is not the answer. Simple, repeatable habits are. "Information is not the problem. Overwhelm is." (Q46, Q59)
- Most people do not need more discipline. They need clarity, realistic systems, and encouragement. (Q47, Q60)
- Budgeting is not just math. It is stress, fear, values, and family dynamics. (Q48)
- Living on one income is hard, and saying so does not mean you are doing it wrong. (Q55)
- Not every season is about optimizing or growing. Sometimes the goal is stability and peace. (Q55)
- You do not have to be extreme, loud, or constantly selling to succeed. (Q58)
- Advice she'd ban: "You just need more discipline." (Q60)
- Drives her crazy: "Just..." advice. "Just cut out everything extra." "Just make more money." "Just stick to the budget." (Q54)
- What changed her mind: she used to think people needed more information; now knows overwhelm is the problem. (Q59)
- Obsessive tracking of every dollar is "busy work that makes you quit after two weeks." "Budget to the level of detail that serves you, not to the level that stresses you out." (AIP Sample 4, Brooke's writing)
- Living below your means is what lets you splurge. A second income should be "extra," not depended on. (ESSAY)

## Words and things she avoids

### Banned words (merge of all sources; the house rules come first)
- **House rules (current, enforced):** hustle, boss babe, girlboss, manifest, abundance mindset. Plus zero em dashes and zero en dashes; ◆ is the only separator; sentence case.
- **BMG 3.5 never-use table:** boss babe, hustle harder, crushing it, six-figure mindset, manifest, abundance mindset, hack, secret, explode your income, lazy, irresponsible, bad with money, get rich quick, overnight success, "You are failing if you do not...", "If you really cared, you would..."
- **Brand prompt never-use:** boss babe, hustle harder, six-figure mindset, manifest, abundance mindset, girlboss, slay, crushing it, leveling up, epic. Plus never long dashes.
- **Sales page prompt:** no hype words like "amazing" or "incredible."
- Brand adjectives to avoid: loud, flashy, salesy, chaotic, judgmental, hype-driven, overwhelming. (BMG Q144)

Conflict: "bad with money" is on BMG's banned list, yet BMG's own transformation statements and the live site use it in negation ("You're not bad with money"). Safe reading: never call the reader bad with money; naming the fear in order to reject it is in use on the site.

Note: the brand guide also says to avoid semicolons and to use exclamation points sparingly (one per email or post max). (BMG 3.4)

### Topics, tactics, and tones she rejects (BMG Appendix E, OSQ Q36)
- Fear, urgency, countdown timers, false scarcity, "buy now or else." (OSQ Q36: "No countdown timers meant to panic people.")
- Shame about where someone is financially. "No shame. No guilt. No 'you should know this by now.'" (OSQ Q36)
- Extremes. "I refuse to teach deprivation disguised as discipline." (OSQ Q36)
- Get-rich-quick, investing advice, early retirement, extreme minimalism, high-income money management. (WB 1.2, OB3)
- Businesses promoting debt, instant gratification, status buying. (BMG Q109)
- Being called a "financial genius" or "money guru." (BMG Q113)
- Overly trendy or staged visuals, neon, dark/moody, Comic Sans. (BMG)
- "Just stop buying lattes" style advice. (WB 3.2, site echoes "No 'just stop buying coffee' advice")
